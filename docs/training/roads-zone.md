# Roads: rain-hidden pothole incident and protected traffic zone

Illustrative training based on generic municipal practice; follow your employer's
SOPs. A human decides every traffic, rescue, medical and reopening action.

**Timebox:** 12–15 minutes guided practice; repeat without hints for assessment.
A short micro-brief and six toolbox/PPE/risk checks precede the street. A rainy
street incident then requires refuge and control-room notification before any
road placement. The same geometry audit remains active throughout.

**Learning outcomes and competencies:** identify concealed pothole depth and poor
visibility; maintain an escape route; report a simulated taxi tyre strike; arrange
traffic accommodation; keep an accessible pedestrian route; use stop/go marshals
from refuges; supplement rigid barriers with hand-placed tape; assess whether EMS
is needed; complete the incident job card and schedule authorised repair.

**Programme and assessment gates:**

1. Theory micro-brief: authority, rain, concealed depth, moving taxis, missing grate,
   traffic speed, banksman and PPE. Correctable answers require remediation; unsafe
   answers save an immediate failed attempt.
2. Confirm personal refuge and escape route. Radio control before unloading.
3. Brief the driver of the already parked cone van; confirm the service bay and
   switch the beacon on. This is simulated acknowledgement, not a driving lesson.
   Place advance warning facing the
   approach; build the six-cone taper and keep-left instruction.
4. Enclose all sides with rigid barriers. Keep the whole footpath clear. Brief
   both stop/go marshals only after van, warning, taper, arrow and rigid protection
   pass. They walk from standby to their refuges with STOP displayed. Credit is
   withheld until arrival and a fresh protection check; changed protection holds
   the crew back for another briefing. Audit every geometry check.
5. From the protected side, pinch the yellow reel at the barrier near x=8 m,
   z=3.65 m. Stretch a 0.8–4 m tape segment to an actual neighbouring barrier;
   release within 0.4 m of that barrier to tie. Short, long, unprotected or unanchored
   spans are refused. The ribbon sags and flaps; tape does not replace barriers.
6. Within protection, check the motorist and the EMS decision. Record structured
   incident/site checks, with no person data, photo capture or media.
7. Keep the closure and schedule the repair crew: cut, clean, tack, fill, compact,
   inspect before reopening. Then run and observe the controlled traffic demonstration.
   Three full-size generic minibus taxis slow at the warning, stop upstream of the
   marshal and proceed on GO in the open lane; 6.5 m queue spacing leaves a gap.
   The two generic marshals use independent rigged bodies with hand-attached
   STOP/GO paddles; only the upstream marshal gestures GO. Reduced motion keeps
   their bodies still and preserves the readable instruction. The crew brief is a simulated instruction; no real worker or dispatcher is
   contacted. The damaged taxi uses a separate downstream bay, clear of both
   marshal routes and the roadside frontage.
8. Debrief every recorded step, safety first, accuracy second, pace last. Retry,
   daily seed, saved practice certification and hub return use the shared engine.

The version-3 rain assessment has **thirteen canonical outcomes**: personal refuge,
radio control, seven traffic checks, hazard tape, motorist/EMS decision, incident
record and repair scheduling. All thirteen must be correct for a pass; omitting an
incident outcome cannot earn a traffic-only certificate. The result card displays
all thirteen checks. Legacy non-rain scenarios retain their seven traffic checks.
Requested hints follow the current rain-incident state.

**Critical fails:** live-lane entry; starting road placement before refuge and radio;
work before advance warning; driver/crew deployment before refuge and radio; knowingly running unsafe traffic geometry; unsafe
pre-shift choices. The supervisor stops work and a non-graphic near-miss/safety
result is saved. Wrong-order incident cards hold progression with feedback and an
accuracy deduction. Moving protection invalidates the previous zone audit.

**Seated path:** hand micro-gesture teleport and snap turn, either-hand pinch/poke;
each prop can be approached by teleport. Carry tape between neighbouring protected
barriers using teleport rather than physical walking. Use reduced motion to show
the damaged taxi already on the shoulder and hold rain/tape motion still. Weather
preference: `?module=roads-zone&weather=clear|drizzle|storm` (storm default).

**Practice ladder:** safety fail awards no pass; corrected procedural work may be
conditional; valid passes feed the shared Assistant/Operator/Supervisor practice record and
refresher-due status. Supervisor refresher links use the documented seed flow.
These labels are practice evidence, not operator authorisation. The displayed ladder is Trainee → Assistant → Operator → Supervisor. Existing
Bronze/Silver/Gold storage values remain compatible; every level is practice evidence.

**Scope limits:** warning/taper distances remain the existing explicitly compressed
training ranges, not verified SARTSM dimensions. Repair is scheduled; physical
cut/clean/tack/fill/compaction and reopening checks are not yet implemented. The
motorist/EMS check is a scripted decision, not clinical triage. Repeated tyre
strikes, pedestrian near-miss choreography and changing traffic escalation remain
unfinished. No operational SOP or complete pothole-repair claim is made.


## South African reference basis

The Department of Transport's published [SARTSM Volume 2, Chapter 13:
Roadworks Signing](https://www.transport.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/V2C13.pdf)
covers temporary signs, traffic management, temporary delineation and urban-street
applications. This is the primary local source for the supervisor's site-plan
review. The [Department's publication index](https://dotevents.transport.gov.za/Test.TransportWeb/roads/)
also lists its Volume 2 material. Source links checked 21 October 2026; confirm the
applicable edition and authorised plan with the road authority. The current
runtime's compressed warning/taper geometry has **not** been mapped to compliant
full-length SARTSM layouts. Do not treat a practice pass as approval of a real
traffic-accommodation design.
