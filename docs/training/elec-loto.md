# Electrical fault response: isolation and personal lockout

Illustrative training based on generic municipal practice; follow your employer's
SOPs. Only an authorised person decides and permits live site work.

**Learning outcomes:** recognise that an outage does not establish isolation;
identify the work-order supply and possible backfeed; apply personal lock/tag;
perform the simulated prove-test-re-prove sequence; communicate remaining hazards.
**Competencies:** supply identification, human notification, securing isolation,
tester verification, deliberate final confirmation and safe handover.
**Timebox:** 8 minutes, with pace feedback after safety and accuracy; no speed bonus.

## Site and shift brief

The unbranded mini-sub is on a municipal street beside a taxi rank, palisade fences,
trader stall and overhead services. An ajar cabinet and tangled unverified service
bundle are hazards to exclude and report. The outage report, possible generator
supply and inaccurate labels require verification. Do not approach, pull or repair
unverified service drops. No fault is a reason to bypass the authority chain.

## Programme sequence and gates

1. (Optional pre-shift briefing, off by default and enabled in title Options.)
   Answer who authorises work; the practice certificate never does.
2. Reject outage-as-isolation; identify and verify supplies.
3. Keep clear of tangled conductors and report to control.
4. Use the approved risk assessment for arc-flash boundaries and PPE rating;
   this scene supplies no universal safe distance or PPE category.
5. Confirm task-rated electrical PPE, visor, gloves and boots are inspected.
6. Reject damaged tester/lock equipment before proceeding.
7. Match SL-3 to the work order and schematic; notify control (offline simulation).
8. Switch the correct supply OFF, place the personal padlock, attach the danger tag,
   then try to switch SL-3 back on: the lock holds it OFF, verifying the isolation.
9. Prove the tester on the proving unit, test the work point, then re-prove.
10. Hold the final safety confirmation; inspect the debrief and local practice result.

The runtime contains ten independently checked procedure steps after preparation.
Requested hints explain the current step and why its prerequisite matters. On a
mislabelled-breaker refresher (`seed=1`), follow the schematic rather than the bad
label. Backfeed exists in the pure procedure; its complete runtime path remains
an explicit verification task, not an assessed release claim.

## Critical fails and remediation

Pulling an unverified conductor or accepting damaged safety equipment instantly
stops preparation and saves a failed attempt. Touching unproven terminals, testing
the wrong circuit, skipping a critical isolation/testing action or declaring safe
too soon stops the field attempt. Keep clear, read the specific feedback, review
with the supervisor and repeat. A corrected critical procedure error does not erase
its safety evidence. Correctable identification errors remain in the accuracy score.

## Seated path and debrief

Read the depot brief with one-hand poke or hand ray/pinch. Enter the full-size
street, then use hands-only teleport and snap turn to the cabinet. The lock, tag and
tester are authored near the seated work point. Final review is positioned at the
current seated view. Reach and all alternative device FoVs require physical Quest
validation; the emulator replay can use longer-than-natural hand travel.

Debrief: explain why loss of supply is not isolation, how the personal lock prevents
re-energisation, and what re-proving establishes. Review safety before accuracy and
pace; use the shared pass/conditional/fail/refresher ladder in [README](README.md).

## Reference basis and limits

South African primary reference: [Electrical Machinery Regulations, 2011,
GN R.250, Gazette 34154](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/34154rg9509gon250.pdf),
reviewed 21 October 2026. Regulation 3 addresses protective equipment; regulation
4 addresses retained/reacquired energy and precautions against re-energisation;
regulation 7(4)–(6) addresses identification and warnings against reclosing locked
or switched-off control gear. These inform the briefing, label check and danger
tag. **Retained-energy/earthing checks are not yet a runtime competency gate.**
The published 2011 text is a source reference, not a claim that this simulation
implements every applicable amendment, standard, authorisation or employer SOP.

The [HSE electrical FAQ](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq.htm) and
[GS38 test-equipment guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/gs38.pdf)
support the general isolation/testing concepts. They are UK guidance, not South
African site authority. Employer-approved SA procedures, training and equipment
instructions must govern any real installation. This simulation does not teach
live work, repair of illegal connections, switching authorisation or certification
under South African electrical regulations.
