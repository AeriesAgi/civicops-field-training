# Water burst response: isolation and repair readiness

Illustrative training based on generic municipal practice; follow your employer's
SOPs. An authorised person decides each work and excavation-entry action.

**Learning outcomes:** protect a flooded live street; interpret the isolation
network; preserve a hospital supply; operate the valve key slowly; verify pressure,
flow and excavation readiness before handover.
**Competencies:** traffic/excavation protection, customer coordination, measured
valve operation, pressure verification and competent-person readiness checks.
**Timebox:** 10 minutes; pace is feedback after safety and accuracy, never a speed bonus.

## Site and shift brief

Muddy water crosses a suburban municipal street with minibus taxis, bins, trader
context and overhead services. A visible burst sits in a shored trench beside
valve chambers. Slippery ground, traffic, water contamination, underground services,
edge loading and changing excavation conditions require continual reassessment.

## Programme sequence and gates

1. Confirm the human authority chain and the site brief.
2. Protect traffic and pedestrians before approaching the flooded trench.
3. Reject entry based only on apparent depth; competent-person checks are essential.
4. Preserve hospital branch C; no unapproved customer interruption.
5. Confirm boots, gloves, eye/high-visibility PPE and hygiene precautions.
6. Inspect the valve key and gauge before use.
7. Hand-place perimeter barriers and warning; move spoil to the illustrative setback.
8. Select the A-B isolation route on the schematic; notify control/customers (simulated).
9. Lift chamber covers, seat the T-key and close A then B using measured wrist rolls.
10. Open washout, confirm low pressure and zero flow.
11. Confirm shoring, ladder/access, spoil, atmosphere and ongoing flooding checks;
    hold repair readiness and review the practice result.

The field procedure contains seven checked steps with multiple physical sub-actions.
The three-turn valves and 0.75 turns/second limit are simulation parameters, not a
prescription for real valves. In `?module=water-burst&seed=1`, seized B requires the
approved A-D plan; the hospital route stays protected. Guidance follows the current
step, including valve seating and incomplete isolation.

## Critical fails and remediation

Unsafe trench entry or unapproved hospital interruption instantly fails preparation.
Operating valves before protection, unauthorised C operation, excessive closure
speed, skipping critical checks or premature repair readiness stops the procedure.
Read the specific stop reason, make safe and repeat after supervisor review.
Wrong route selection is held for correction; the error remains in the score.
Reopening an isolation valve invalidates prior isolation/readiness checks.

## Seated path and debrief

Use one hand for covers and key; regrip rather than twisting beyond wrist comfort.
Teleport to each valve chamber with hands-only locomotion; never require crawling
or physical trench entry. Initial Water spawn, seat tolerances and all valve reaches
remain under seated review; the current emulator replay is not a comfort pass.

Debrief: explain which customers stay supplied, why valve closure alone does not
prove zero pressure, and who can permit excavation entry. Review safety, accuracy,
then pace with the [shared assessment ladder](README.md).

## Current scope and completion boundary

The programme currently assesses **isolation and repair readiness**. Clamp fitting,
cross-pattern torque, approved slow recommissioning, leak testing and reinstatement
are required future programme extensions. They are not interactive or assessed yet;
a pass must not be described as completed pipe-repair competence.

[HSE excavation guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/construction/safetytopics/excavations.htm)
and [HSG47](https://www.hse.gov.uk/PuBns/books/hsg47.htm) support the general hazards
of excavation, water ingress and underground services. These are UK references, not
South African employer SOPs. Real support design, isolation, entry and repair need
competent site planning and approved local procedures.
